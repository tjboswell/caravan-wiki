# Drop incremental hub for thin unlock pool

> The scaling production hub is cut. Persistence moves to a thin, Slay-the-Spire-style **unlock pool**: clear runs, unlock new content into the pool, run again.

## Reasoning

The incremental hub was the single biggest source of scope, complexity, and unanswered questions — and the designer already treated it as vestigial ("the run should be fun without the hub," "the hub is just a quick pit stop"). Cutting it collapses three coupled systems (run economy, hub economy, the coupling) down to one game plus a cheap unlock layer.

The two feelings the hub provided move elsewhere: the **incremental "watch it scale"** sensation relocates *inside the run* (the [[systems/Heat]] multiply/spread snowball), and **persistence / consequence** becomes the [[systems/Unlock Pool]]. What's genuinely lost is the *idle-hum* and the *merchant-empire* fantasy; the merchant flavor can return later as a purely cosmetic home-base skin over the unlock pool (Hades-style), with no simulation. That's a deferred, zero-mechanical-risk maybe.

## Rejected

- **Incremental production hub** — a second full game; endless balance/coupling questions.
- **Idle scaling engine** — required real-time simulation; wrong emotional register once the run carries the snowball.
- **Materials coupling** — see [[decisions/Points are score and shop currency]].

## Affects

- [[systems/The Hub]] — superseded
- [[systems/Unlock Pool]] — the thin persistence that replaces it
- [[systems/The Run]] — now the whole game, not one of two halves
