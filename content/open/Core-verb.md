# Core verb

> What is the player's primary verb — the thing done dozens of times a run — that climbs cargo value?

## The question

The game needs a central, repeatable action that feels native to it. The current placeholder is conversion (Century-style), but the right answer is unresolved.

## Candidates

**(a) Conversion** — trade up: 2× T1 → 1× T2. Current placeholder. The Century exposure: if conversion is the verb, the game structurally resembles Century regardless of what's stacked on top.

**(b) Market / geography** — value comes from *place + timing*. A good is worth more at the right town at the right moment. The route *becomes* the engine; playing well means reading demand across space and time. Structurally furthest from Century.

**(c) Synergy** — the engine transforms goods as a side-effect of triggering combinations. You build a machine of interlocking cards; cargo climbs as a consequence of the machine running. Most Balatro-like.

## Sub-fork: intrinsic vs. place-based value

Is a good's value **intrinsic** (tier-based — T1 < T2 < T3 regardless of context) or **purely place-based** (a commodity market where value is set by local supply and demand)? This sub-question is tied to which candidate wins.

## Driving constraint

**"Must not feel like Century with bells and whistles."** Conversion is the exposure. If conversion stays as the primary verb, the surrounding systems (gates, relics, area escalation) need to be doing enough work to make it feel genuinely different — but that's a risky bet.

## Why it's deliberately unresolved

The gate / relic / area economy was designed to sit on top of *any* of these verbs, which is why those systems could be built without resolving this question first. But it cannot stay open forever — the core-verb shapes every other system's feel.
