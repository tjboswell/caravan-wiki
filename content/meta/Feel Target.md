# Feel Target

> The four emotional registers the game must hit.

## The targets

**[[references/Balatro]]'s "it's going exponential" grin**
The run builds into something. Your engine of merchant cards goes from thin and improvised to a humming, chaining machine. Late in a run you should feel the acceleration — conversions chaining into conversions, cargo upgrading fast. The player leans forward.

**[[references/Century Spice Road|Century's]] satisfying cargo-conversion engine**
The moment-to-moment feel of trading goods is satisfying in itself — not just for the outcome, but for the act. Matching a card's conversion to your cargo, hitting the payout on a contract, watching goods transform. The mechanics should feel good to *do*, not just to *complete*.

**[[references/Gnorp Apologue|Gnorp's]] hypnotic watch-it-scale**
The hub running is pleasurable to observe. Materials flowing in, buildings processing, goods accumulating. You should be happy to leave it running while you do something else — and mildly excited to come back and see how hungry it's gotten.

**A merchant's "read the market, time the commitment" nerve**
The run has tension. Which contracts to chase. When to invest in a new card vs. run what you have. Whether to push for one more town or head home with what you've got. This is the skill register — not combat skill, but *read-the-situation* skill.
