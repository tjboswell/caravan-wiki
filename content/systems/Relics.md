# Relics

> Persistent run modifiers bought in towns — a distinct power channel from merchant cards, forming the run's compounding engine.

## How it works

Relics are permanent upgrades that ride with you across the entire run. They are a **separate axis of power** from [[systems/Merchant Cards|merchant cards]] and jokers — two independent ways to get stronger, not two names for the same thing.

**Bought in towns, never at gates.** Specifically available in Market and Offer towns. No relic rewards at [[systems/Gates|gates]].

**Cost: cargo.** Mid-tier cargo — the same goods the gate would sell at a good price. Every relic purchase directly dents the sale you need *this* gate, betting the modifier pays off across *future* gates. That invest-vs-survive gut-check is the tension the system exists to create.

**Capped slots (currently 4-in-pool / 3-slots).** You encounter more relics than you can hold, so keeping one means passing others. The slot cap turns your relic choices into a *build* and decouples how-many-you-see from how-strong-you-get — this keeps the ante floors tunable without constantly adjusting the relic economy.

**Must compound, not add.** Relics must operate as multipliers or triggers, not flat bonuses. Because gate floors scale like antes, flat additions lose to the curve. The compounding relic build barely outrunning the ever-rising bar is the Balatro-grin engine.

## Why relics are the progression spine

[[systems/Cargo|Cargo]] resets at every gate (sold, then restocked). Relics persist. This split — cargo resets / engine persists — *is* the run's progression curve, and is a main reason the game pulls away from the Century feel: you're not just optimizing goods, you're building a permanent machine that gets hungrier to feed but more powerful if you do.
